Resume Format Guide · India

How to format a resume for Indian job applications

A strong resume format makes the important facts easy to find: who you are, what role you fit, what you have done, and what evidence supports your skills. The goal is clarity, not decoration.

Quick answer: use a clean reverse-chronological structure, consistent dates, readable headings, standard contact details and one or two pages depending on experience. Put your most relevant evidence near the top and avoid information the employer did not request.

Recommended section order

There is no single mandatory order for every profession, but most Indian job applications work well with a predictable sequence. Recruiters should not have to hunt for education, experience or contact information.

  1. Name and contact details: phone, professional email, city, and relevant links such as LinkedIn, GitHub or portfolio.
  2. Professional summary: two to four lines explaining your target role, relevant experience or strengths, and the evidence that makes you credible.
  3. Work experience: newest role first. Use concise bullets that explain actions, scope and outcomes.
  4. Education: degree, institution, location if useful, and graduation year. Freshers can move education above experience.
  5. Projects: especially important for students, developers, designers, analysts and career switchers.
  6. Skills: tools, technologies, methods and relevant capabilities that you can actually demonstrate.
  7. Optional sections: certifications, awards, publications, languages or volunteering when they strengthen the application.
Fresher variation: Name & contact → Summary → Education → Projects → Internship/Experience → Skills → Certifications/Achievements. This puts the strongest available proof before an empty or short employment history.

One page or two pages?

A one-page resume is useful when you can present all relevant information without shrinking the font or deleting meaningful evidence. Many freshers and early-career applicants can do this. A two-page resume is reasonable when several years of relevant work, projects or leadership responsibilities would otherwise be compressed into vague bullets.

Do not force a senior profile onto one page just because someone online says every resume must be one page. At the same time, do not use a second page to repeat responsibilities, list every course taken, or add unrelated personal details. Each line should help the employer evaluate fit.

Contact details: what to include

Place your name at the top in the largest text on the page. Use a phone number you actively answer and an email address that looks professional. City and state are usually enough; a full residential address is rarely necessary for an online application unless the employer explicitly requests it.

Include LinkedIn, GitHub, Behance, portfolio or another professional link only when the destination is updated and relevant. Check that every link opens correctly before sending the resume.

What about photo, date of birth or marital status?

For most modern private-sector applications, these details are not necessary unless a particular employer, form, industry or country asks for them. A photo can be appropriate for some roles or regional conventions, but it should not consume space needed for qualifications. Never add sensitive personal information simply because an old resume template contains a field for it.

Use consistent dates and chronology

Choose one date style and use it everywhere: for example, Jan 2024 – Present or 01/2024 – Present. Do not switch among “January”, “Jan”, “2024”, and “1/24” across sections. Consistency makes the timeline easier to understand and reduces accidental ambiguity.

Use reverse chronology for experience and education unless another format is genuinely more useful. If you have an employment gap, you do not need to hide it by manipulating dates. A short, honest explanation in an interview is safer than a timeline that looks inconsistent.

Keep the layout ATS-friendly

Applicant tracking systems vary, so no template can guarantee parsing. You can still reduce unnecessary risk by using clear headings such as “Experience”, “Education”, “Projects” and “Skills”; real text instead of text embedded inside images; readable font sizes; and simple date formats.

Two-column layouts can look good and often parse correctly, but a conservative single-column layout is the safest choice when you know the employer relies heavily on automated screening or when the job portal already asks you to upload structured information separately.

Avoid formatting that hides information

  • Do not put important contact information only in a decorative graphic.
  • Avoid progress bars for skills such as “Excel 85%” unless the percentage has a real, explainable meaning.
  • Do not use tiny fonts to force everything onto one page.
  • Keep section names conventional unless the creative label is obvious.
  • Export a real PDF and inspect every page break before applying.

Format experience for fast scanning

Each role should show company, title, dates and location when relevant. Under that, use bullets that explain what you did and why it mattered. A useful bullet often contains an action, context and result.

Weak: “Responsible for social media.”
Stronger: “Planned the weekly LinkedIn content calendar for a B2B team and coordinated 3 contributors, reducing missed publishing deadlines.”

The stronger version is not better because it is longer. It is better because it tells the reader what work was actually performed. Add metrics only when you know they are accurate.

Formatting for freshers

If you do not have formal employment, do not leave half the page empty waiting for “Experience”. Projects, internships, volunteering, coursework and campus responsibilities can show relevant ability. Describe them with the same discipline you would use for a job: what was the goal, what did you personally do, what tools did you use, and what was the outcome?

A software fresher might show two strong projects with repository links. A marketing fresher might show a college campaign, internship or event promotion. A finance fresher might show spreadsheet modelling, a research project or a relevant certification. The format should make those signals easy to find.

Final format checklist

  • One clear H1-style name, then conventional section headings.
  • Phone, email and professional links tested.
  • Dates use one format and the chronology makes sense.
  • Most relevant evidence appears on page one.
  • Every skill listed is something you can explain in an interview.
  • Spelling of employers, institutions and qualifications is correct.
  • No clipped text, broken bullets or awkward page breaks in the PDF.
  • Filename is professional, such as Priya-Verma-Resume.pdf.
